Asana is good at something specific: making fifty projects legible to someone who is not working on any of them. Portfolios, goals, workload balancing, approval flows. If you are a program manager, that is worth paying for.

If you are a team of nine using it as a shared task list, you are paying $10.99 per user a month, minimum, for a fraction of that. And when your company adopts an identity provider and you go looking for SAML single sign-on, you will find it on the Enterprise tier.

That gap, between what Asana is built for and what most teams use it for, is why people go looking. This is a comparison of the 10 tools worth considering, including the free and self-hostable ones that most round-ups skip. Prices were checked in August 2026, so confirm on the vendor's own page before you commit.

## Why teams look for an Asana alternative

**Per-seat pricing that scales the wrong way.** At $10.99 per user a month, a fifteen-person team is roughly $165 a month, or about $2,000 a year, for project tracking. That is defensible if you use the portfolio and goals layer. It is hard to defend if you use boards and due dates.

**The free tier is small.** Asana's free plan caps collaborators at a level most real teams pass quickly, which turns "let's try it" into a purchase decision faster than you expect.

**Single sign-on is an Enterprise feature.** SAML sits on Enterprise, which is quote-only. This is the industry norm rather than an Asana quirk, but it is the point where a lot of teams reassess. We track [which project management tools include SSO for free](/guides/project-management-tools-with-free-sso) if that is your constraint.

**No self-hosting, at all.** Asana is cloud only. If you have a data residency requirement, a compliance obligation, or simply want to own the database, there is no configuration that gets you there.

**Feature weight you do not use.** Goals, portfolios, workload, and approvals are genuinely good and genuinely irrelevant to a nine-person team shipping software.

### 2. ClickUp

ClickUp is the broadest tool on this list: tasks, docs, whiteboards, chat, dashboards, goals, and time tracking in one product. If your objection to Asana is price rather than scope, ClickUp gives you more for less.

**Key features:** Dozens of views, docs, whiteboards, chat, dashboards, goals, automations, native time tracking.

**Pros:** Enormous feature surface, generous free tier, cheaper per seat than Asana, one subscription instead of several.

**Cons:** Cloud only and proprietary. The breadth is genuinely overwhelming for a small team, and someone has to configure it properly. SAML single sign-on sits on the Business tier and above.

**Pricing:** Paid tiers from $7 per user a month. See [ClickUp's pricing](https://clickup.com/pricing).

**Best for:** Teams who actually want the docs, whiteboards, and chat, not just the tasks.

**Why choose it over Asana:** More capability at a lower per-seat price. Compare with [Kaneo vs ClickUp](/clickup-alternative) if the breadth is the part you would turn off.

### 3. monday.com

monday.com is a work OS rather than a tracker. Colourful boards, dashboards, forms, automations, and CRM templates, aimed squarely at operations, marketing, and sales teams.

**Key features:** Customisable boards, dashboards, forms, automation recipes, CRM and HR templates, an app marketplace.

**Pros:** Highly visual and approachable for non-technical teams, does far more than task tracking, strong template library.

**Cons:** Cloud only, proprietary, sold in seat blocks rather than exact headcount, and single sign-on is an Enterprise-tier feature. The free tier is very limited.

**Pricing:** Per user with seat minimums. See [monday.com's pricing](https://monday.com/pricing).

**Best for:** Non-engineering teams who want dashboards and forms alongside their work.

**Why choose it over Asana:** More flexible board structures and a stronger automation story for operations work. See [Kaneo vs monday.com](/monday-alternative) for the self-hosted comparison.

### 4. Trello

Trello is the simplest board that works. If Asana feels like too much structure, Trello is the opposite end of that spectrum, and its free tier is genuinely usable.

**Key features:** Drag-and-drop boards, cards, checklists, due dates, Power-Ups for anything beyond the basics.

**Pros:** Almost no learning curve, strong free tier, cheap paid tiers, universally understood.

**Cons:** Cloud only and proprietary. No backlog, no real workflow model, and anything beyond a board means Power-Ups. Single sign-on is Enterprise-tier.

**Pricing:** Free tier, then per user.

**Best for:** Small teams and personal projects that need a board and nothing else.

**Why choose it over Asana:** Dramatically simpler and cheaper. If you want that simplicity on your own server, see [Kaneo vs Trello](/trello-alternative) or our guide to [self-hosted Trello alternatives](/guides/self-hosted-trello-alternatives).

### 5. Notion

Notion is a flexible workspace for documents, wikis, and lightweight databases. Plenty of teams run projects in it by building a task database, and for the right team that works well.

**Key features:** Documents and wikis, databases with board, table, calendar, and timeline views, templates, and an increasingly capable automation layer.

**Pros:** Genuinely excellent at documentation, extremely flexible, one place for notes and tasks.

**Cons:** Cloud only and proprietary. Project tracking is something you build and maintain rather than something you get, and it tends to drift. Single sign-on starts at the Business tier.

**Pricing:** Per member per month. See [Notion's pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing).

**Best for:** Teams whose work lives in written documents, where the task list is a side effect.

**Why choose it over Asana:** One tool for docs and light tracking instead of two subscriptions. Compare with [Kaneo vs Notion](/notion-alternative) if the database has started getting in the way.

### 6. Basecamp

Basecamp charges a flat monthly fee rather than per user, which is a genuinely good deal for larger teams and an unusual position in this market.

**Key features:** Message boards, to-do lists, docs and files, group chat, automatic check-ins, and client access.

**Pros:** Flat pricing that does not scale with headcount, opinionated and calm, strong client-facing features.

**Cons:** Cloud only and proprietary. Organised around message boards and to-do lists rather than a kanban board and backlog. No single sign-on.

**Pricing:** Flat rate, not per user. See [Basecamp's pricing](https://basecamp.com/pricing).

**Best for:** Companies whose project tool is also their internal communication tool, especially agencies with client access needs.

**Why choose it over Asana:** Predictable cost at any team size, and a much calmer product. See [Kaneo vs Basecamp](/basecamp-alternative) if you want the board and backlog back.

### 7. Wrike

Wrike is aimed at larger marketing, professional services, and operations teams: request forms, proofing, resource allocation, and time-and-budget reporting in one place.

**Key features:** Request forms, proofing and approvals, resource management, time and budget reporting, Gantt charts, custom item types.

**Pros:** The most complete option here for billable client work at scale, with genuine resource management.

**Cons:** Cloud-first and proprietary, priced per user from $10 a month with plan-specific user ranges that make the tier maths awkward. Single sign-on sits on higher tiers.

**Pricing:** From $10 per user a month.

**Best for:** Operations and professional services teams running billable work.

**Why choose it over Asana:** Stronger proofing and resource allocation. See [Kaneo vs Wrike](/wrike-alternative) if you want one feature set instead of user bands.

### 8. Linear

Linear is the fastest and most polished tracker in the category. It is not an Asana replacement for marketing teams, but for a product or engineering team leaving Asana, it is often the right destination.

**Key features:** Cycles, triage inbox, projects and initiatives, insights, keyboard-first navigation, deep GitHub and Slack integrations.

**Pros:** Exceptional speed and polish, excellent engineering workflow, no configuration overhead.

**Cons:** Cloud only, proprietary, opinionated in a way that suits software teams and few others. Single sign-on on paid plans.

**Pricing:** Per user, cloud only. See [Linear's pricing](https://linear.app/pricing).

**Best for:** Product and engineering teams who value speed over breadth.

**Why choose it over Asana:** Far better suited to shipping software. See [Kaneo vs Linear](/linear-alternative) for the self-hostable version of that idea.

### 9. Plane

Plane is an open-source tracker with a modern interface, close in shape to Linear. The Community edition is AGPL-3.0 and free to self-host.

**Key features:** Cycles, modules, intake, pages, issue hierarchy, self-hosted Community edition.

**Pros:** Genuinely good interface, actively developed, free Community edition, no per-seat cost when self-hosted.

**Cons:** AGPL-3.0 has real implications if you plan to modify and redistribute it. Single sign-on starts at the Pro tier. The self-hosted stack is larger than a single container.

**Pricing:** Community edition free to self-host, paid cloud tiers. See [Plane's pricing](https://plane.so/pricing).

**Best for:** Engineering teams who want a modern tracker on their own infrastructure.

**Why choose it over Asana:** No per-seat pricing and full data ownership. Compare with [Kaneo vs Plane](/plane-alternative) on deployment weight and licensing.

### 10. OpenProject

OpenProject is the most complete open-source option for classical project management: Gantt charts, baselines, budgets, cost reporting, and work-package hierarchies.

**Key features:** Gantt charts and baselines, work packages, budgets and cost reporting, agile boards, time and cost tracking, commercial support.

**Pros:** Free Community edition, mature and well documented, real support offering, the only option here with proper budget tracking.

**Cons:** The Community edition is GPLv3 and reserves single sign-on and custom branding for the Enterprise add-on. Cloud Enterprise plans start at a 25-user minimum. It is a platform to operate, not a small app.

**Pricing:** Community edition free to self-host, Enterprise add-on priced separately.

**Best for:** Teams that need Gantt charts, budgets, and cost reporting, and want to own the deployment.

**Why choose it over Asana:** Comparable planning depth without per-seat pricing. See [Kaneo vs OpenProject](/openproject-alternative) for the lighter trade.

## Migrating off Asana

Asana has a CSV export per project and a documented REST API, so a scripted migration is realistic. What tends to break is subtask nesting, custom fields, and attachment history.

Export one active project first and import it somewhere as a test. Check that assignees resolve by email, that due dates land in the right timezone, and that comment authorship survives. Keep Asana readable for a quarter rather than cancelling the same week. Kaneo has a public REST API and per-project JSON import, and we will help with a migration script if you open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/usekaneo/kaneo).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free alternative to Asana?

Trello's free tier is the most usable of the hosted free plans, and ClickUp's is the most generous in features. If you are willing to run a server, Kaneo is MIT licensed and self-hostable. Most teams that price out the server, the upgrades, and the backups end up on a managed plan instead, and Kaneo Cloud at $5 per user a month is roughly half of Asana's entry tier.

### Is there an open-source alternative to Asana?

Yes. Kaneo (MIT), Plane (AGPL-3.0 Community edition), and OpenProject (GPLv3 Community edition) are the main options, and all three can be self-hosted for free. They differ in scope and in what the free build withholds, particularly around single sign-on.

### Can Asana be self-hosted?

No. Asana is cloud only, with no on-premise or self-managed option at any tier. If self-hosting is a requirement, you need a different tool rather than a different plan.

### Which Asana alternative is cheapest for a large team?

Among managed options, Basecamp's flat rate wins at genuine scale, and Kaneo Cloud at $5 per user a month is roughly half of Asana's entry tier with single sign-on included rather than gated. Self-hosting an open-source tool is cheaper still on paper, because the bill is a server rather than a headcount, as long as you have someone to run it.

### Does Asana include single sign-on?

SAML single sign-on is an Enterprise-tier feature on Asana. Google sign-in is available more broadly, which covers many small teams. If you need SAML or OIDC against your own identity provider without an Enterprise contract, check our [guide to tools with free SSO](/guides/project-management-tools-with-free-sso).

### What does Asana do that these alternatives do not?

Portfolios, goals, and workload views. If a program manager needs fifty projects rolled into one status view, with capacity across people, Asana does that properly and most of this list does not. That is the feature to check for before you move.
